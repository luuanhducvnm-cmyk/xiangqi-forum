*Updated 19 November 2026. The match has not started yet at the time of writing; check the official FIDE site for live results.*

The 2026 World Chess Championship match pairs reigning champion **Gukesh Dommaraju** of India with challenger **Javokhir Sindarov** of Uzbekistan. It is played at the Fondation Martin Bodmer in Cologny, Geneva, Switzerland. Here are the facts as FIDE published them.

## Schedule

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 22 November 2026 | Media day and opening ceremony |
| 24 November 2026 | First classical game |
| 11 December 2026 | Last scheduled classical game |
| 12 December 2026 | Tiebreaks, if the match is level |

## Format and rules

- Up to **14 classical games**; the first player to **7.5 points** wins the title.
- Time control: 120 minutes for the first 40 moves, then 30 minutes for the rest of the game, with a 30-second increment from move 41.
- Draw offers are not allowed before Black's 40th move; before that a draw can only be claimed through the arbiters for threefold repetition or stalemate.
- If the score is 7-7: four rapid games (15 minutes + 10 seconds), then two games at 10+5, then two blitz games at 3+2, then sudden-death blitz games with colours reversed after each draw.

## Prize fund

The fund is US$2.5 million, net of local taxes. Each game win earns US$200,000 and the rest is split equally. If the match ends 7.5-6.5 with 13 decisive games, or 8-6 with all 14 decisive, the winner receives US$1.3 million and the runner-up US$1.2 million. The same 1.3 / 1.2 split applies if the match is decided in tiebreaks.

## Who are the players?

- **Gukesh Dommaraju** (India) is the reigning World Champion. He is rated **2710** on the November 2026 FIDE list and ranked 26th, according to Chess.com.
- **Javokhir Sindarov** (Uzbekistan) won the 2026 Candidates Tournament with 10 points out of 14 to earn the match. He is rated **2782**, fourth in the world, **72 points** above the champion. Chess.com's report on the November list calls the 22-place ranking gap a record for a title match since 2006.

On paper Sindarov is the favourite; ratings, however, are only a guide and match play over 14 classical games is different from a tournament. See the [full top 10](/news/fide-rating-list-november-2026/) for the current list.
