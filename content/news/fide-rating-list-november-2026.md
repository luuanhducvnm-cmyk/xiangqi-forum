*Updated 19 November 2026. Based on the FIDE classical rating list published at the start of November 2026.*

Magnus Carlsen of Norway still leads the world on the **November 2026 FIDE classical list** with **2823**, according to Chess.com's report by Colin McGourty. Here is the top 10.

## What stands out

- **The champion is not in the top 10.** Gukesh Dommaraju, the reigning World Champion, is rated 2710 and ranked 26th. His challenger Javokhir Sindarov is fourth at 2782, a gap of 72 points and 22 places that Chess.com describes as a record for a title match since 2006. Read the [match preview](/news/world-chess-championship-2026/).
- **Nihal Sarin** gained 11 points to reach 2720 and a career-best 19th place after strong results at the European Club Cup.
- **Hans Niemann** lost 20 points to 2697 (world number 32) and dropped out of the 2700 club after a weak U.S. Championship.
- **Women's list:** Alice Lee rose to 2474 (14th in the world) and is now the top U.S. woman; Carissa Yip fell to 2453; Rachael Li, the U.S. Women's Championship winner, gained 50 points to 2430. Bodhana Sivanandan earned the IM title and is rated 2414.

## How ratings work

FIDE uses an Elo-style system: win against a stronger opponent and you gain more points. A 72-point gap means the higher-rated player is expected to score about 60 per cent in a game. Our [ELO explainer](/elo-rating-explained/) shows the maths, and the [time controls guide](/chess/time-controls/) explains why classical, rapid and blitz ratings differ.
