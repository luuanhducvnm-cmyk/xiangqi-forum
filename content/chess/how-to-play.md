Chess looks complicated, but the rules fit on a single page. This guide takes you from the board to your first game.

## The board and setup

The chessboard has 64 squares in an 8×8 grid, alternating light and dark. Place it so that each player has a **light square on their right-hand corner**. Each player has 16 pieces:

{{img:ch-start|The starting position. White is at the bottom and moves first.}}

- The back rank: rook, knight, bishop, **queen**, **king**, bishop, knight, rook. The queen goes on her own colour: the white queen on a light square, the black queen on a dark square.
- The second rank: eight pawns.

## How the pieces move

| Piece | Moves |
|---|---|
| King | One square in any direction |
| Queen | Any number of squares along a rank, file or diagonal |
| Rook | Any number of squares along a rank or file |
| Bishop | Any number of squares diagonally |
| Knight | In an L-shape: two squares one way and one square at right angles; it can jump over pieces |
| Pawn | Forward one square (two from its starting square); captures one square diagonally forward |

See [the pieces page](/chess/pieces/) for diagrams, and the [special moves page](/chess/special-moves/) for castling, en passant and promotion.

## Capturing

You capture by moving a piece onto a square occupied by an enemy piece, which is removed from the board. Pawns are the exception: they move straight but capture diagonally. You can never capture your own pieces.

## Check and checkmate

- **Check**: the king is attacked. You must get out of check immediately, by moving the king, capturing the attacker, or blocking the line of attack (not possible against a knight).
- **Checkmate**: the king is in check and no legal move escapes. The game is over and the player giving checkmate wins.
- **Stalemate**: the player to move is not in check but has no legal move. The game is a draw.

More on endings in [how games end](/chess/ways-to-end/) and the most useful [checkmate patterns](/chess/checkmates/).

## A plan for your first game

1. **Open with a centre pawn** (`e4` or `d4`).
2. **Develop** your knights and bishops toward the centre.
3. **Castle** early to put your king in safety ([how castling works](/chess/special-moves/)).
4. **Connect** your rooks and bring them to open files.
5. **Do not move the same piece twice** in the opening without a reason, and do not bring out your queen too early.
6. **Look for threats** before every move: "what is my opponent threatening?".

## Beginner mistakes to avoid

- Hanging pieces — leaving a piece where it can be captured for nothing.
- Moving too many pawns, or too early.
- Bringing the queen out early, where she can be chased.
- Ignoring the opponent's last move.
- Forgetting to castle.

## Where to practise

Play against the Royal AI or other players in [Chess 3D](https://chess3d.online), where you can tap a piece to see where it can go and play with a real two-button chess clock.
