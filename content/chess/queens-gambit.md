The **Queen's Gambit** starts **1.d4 d5 2.c4**. White attacks the d5 pawn from the side and tries to fight for the centre. It is a classic of positional chess, and a good start for players who like clear plans. **Every diagram was checked by playing the moves in the Chess 3D rules engine.**

## Black's three main replies

### Queen's Gambit Declined (2...e6)

Black holds the centre solidly. A typical line is **3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Bg5 Be7 5.e3 O-O**.

{{img:ln-qgd|Queen's Gambit Declined: 1.d4 d5 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Bg5 Be7 5.e3 O-O.}}

Black's pieces are slightly cramped, particularly the bishop on c8, but the pawn structure is sound.

### Queen's Gambit Accepted (2...dxc4)

Black takes the pawn. White will recapture, so Black should not try to hold on to it.

{{img:ln-qga|Queen's Gambit Accepted: 3.Nf3 Nf6 4.e3 e6 5.Bxc4 c5.}}

After 5.Bxc4 Black has given the pawn back and can strike with ...c5.

### Slav Defence (2...c6)

Black defends d5 with the c-pawn so the light-squared bishop can leave the centre.

{{img:ln-slav|Slav Defence: 2...c6, then ...dxc4 to free the bishop.}}

## Plans for both sides

- **White:** control the centre with pawns on d4 and c4, develop the knights and bishops, and use the open c-file when pawns are exchanged.
- **Black:** keep the pawn on d5 or give it back at the right time, solve the problem of the bishop on c8 and strike with ...c5 or ...e5.

## Why it is good for learning

The positions are about structure and plans, not tricks. You learn when to exchange pawns, how to use open files and how to place pieces. For the opposite style, learn the [Sicilian Defence](/chess/sicilian-defence/) or the [Italian Game](/chess/italian-game/). Practise on [Chess 3D](https://chess3d.online).
