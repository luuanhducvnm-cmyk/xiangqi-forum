Chess has three special moves that confuse beginners. None of them is hard once you see the exact conditions.

## Castling

Castling is the only move in which two pieces move at once: the king moves two squares toward a rook and the rook jumps to the other side of the king. It is the best way to make your king safe and to bring a rook into the game.

{{img:ch-castle|White can castle kingside (king to g1, rook to f1) or queenside (king to c1, rook to d1).}}

**Conditions — all must be true:**

1. Neither the king nor that rook has moved earlier in the game.
2. All squares between them are empty.
3. The king is not in check.
4. The king does not pass through, or land on, an attacked square.

Kingside castling is written `O-O`; queenside is `O-O-O` ([notation](/chess/notation/)). A rook may be attacked or have been attacked; only the king's squares matter.

## En passant

"En passant" is French for "in passing". When a pawn advances two squares from its starting rank and lands **beside** an enemy pawn, the enemy pawn may capture it **as if it had moved only one square**.

{{img:ch-enpassant|Black has just played d7–d5. White's pawn on e5 can capture it en passant, landing on d6.}}

**Rules:**

- It is only possible directly after the two-square advance; if you do not take it immediately, the right is lost.
- The capturing pawn lands on the square the enemy pawn skipped over.
- It is only available to a pawn on its fifth rank (for White) or fourth rank (for Black).

## Promotion

When a pawn reaches the last rank (the eighth for White, the first for Black), it **must** be exchanged for a queen, rook, bishop or knight of the same colour.

{{img:ch-promotion|The white pawn on e7 is one step from promotion on e8.}}

- You choose the piece; the most common choice is a queen.
- **Underpromotion** (choosing a rook, bishop or knight) is occasionally better — for example to give a knight check or to avoid stalemate.
- You can have two or more queens.

## Common mistakes

- Trying to castle through check.
- Forgetting that en passant must be played immediately.
- Waiting too long to promote because you did not notice the pawn had reached the last rank.
- Castling into a pawn storm — castling early is good, but the side you castle to matters.
