These are the rules of chess as used in nearly all casual and online games. They follow the FIDE Laws of Chess, simplified for readability.

## 1. Setup

The board is 8×8, with a light square at each player's right-hand corner. White places pieces on ranks 1 and 2; Black on ranks 7 and 8. The queen starts on her own colour. White moves first, then players alternate.

## 2. The pieces

Each side has one king, one queen, two rooks, two bishops, two knights and eight pawns. Movements are summarised below and illustrated on [the pieces page](/chess/pieces/).

| Piece | Movement |
|---|---|
| King | One square in any direction. |
| Queen | Any distance along a rank, file or diagonal. |
| Rook | Any distance along a rank or file. |
| Bishop | Any distance diagonally. |
| Knight | L-shape (2+1); jumps over pieces. |
| Pawn | Forward one square (two on its first move); captures diagonally forward. |

All pieces except the knight cannot jump over other pieces.

## 3. Capturing

A piece captures by moving onto a square occupied by an enemy piece. The captured piece is removed. You may never capture your own pieces, and a king is never captured.

## 4. Check, checkmate and legal moves

A king is in **check** when it is attacked. When in check you must immediately:

1. move the king to a safe square,
2. capture the attacking piece, or
3. block the attack (except against a knight check, which cannot be blocked).

You may never make a move that leaves your own king in check. If you are in check and have no legal move, it is **checkmate**.

## 5. Special moves

- **Castling** — the king moves two squares toward a rook and the rook jumps over it. Conditions: neither piece has moved; no pieces between them; the king is not in check, does not pass through an attacked square, and does not land on one.
- **En passant** — a pawn that has just advanced two squares can be captured by an adjacent enemy pawn as if it had moved only one square, but only on the very next move.
- **Promotion** — a pawn that reaches the last rank must become a queen, rook, bishop or knight (almost always a queen).

Details and diagrams: [special moves](/chess/special-moves/).

## 6. Ways the game ends

| Result | When |
|---|---|
| Win by checkmate | The king is in check and cannot escape. |
| Win by resignation | A player resigns. |
| Win on time | The opponent's clock runs out (unless you cannot possibly checkmate; then the game is drawn). |
| Draw by stalemate | The player to move has no legal move and is not in check. |
| Draw by agreement | Both players agree. |
| Draw by repetition | The same position occurs three times with the same player to move. |
| Draw by the fifty-move rule | 50 moves by each side with no pawn move and no capture. |
| Draw by insufficient material | Neither side can possibly checkmate (for example king versus king). |

See [how chess games end](/chess/ways-to-end/) for examples.

## 7. Conduct and the clock

- Moves are made one at a time.
- In timed games, each player has a clock; you press your side's button after your move.
- In over-the-board play, a touched piece must be moved (touch-move) and a piece you let go of on a new square stays there.
- Disturbing the opponent, using outside help or computers is not allowed in rated games.
