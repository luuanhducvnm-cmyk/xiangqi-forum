The **Ruy Lopez** (also called the Spanish Game) starts **1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5**. The bishop attacks the knight that defends the e5 pawn. White is not threatening to win a pawn at once, but is building pressure. It has been a main weapon at the highest level for a very long time. **Every diagram here was verified by the Chess 3D rules engine.**

## The Closed Variation

After **3...a6 4.Ba4 Nf6 5.O-O Be7 6.Re1 b5 7.Bb3 d6 8.c3 O-O** both sides have completed their development, and the typical structure of the Closed Ruy Lopez appears.

{{img:ln-ruy-closed|Closed Ruy Lopez after 8...O-O: the classic main-line setup.}}

Key plans:
- **White:** prepare the central break d4, usually with h3 and Nbd2. A pawn move in the centre decides the game.
- **Black:** keep solid, often with ...Na5 and ...c5 to strike back, or ...Bb7 to pressure e4.
- The bishop on b3 stays on the a2-g8 diagonal and pressures f7.

## The Exchange Variation

White can play **4.Bxc6 dxc6**, doubling Black's c-pawns. Black gets the bishop pair; White has a healthy 4 versus 3 pawn majority on the kingside, which counts in endgames.

{{img:ln-ruy-exchange|Exchange Variation: 4.Bxc6 dxc6, doubled pawns for Black and an endgame plan for White.}}

## What to learn from the Ruy Lopez

- **Pressure instead of immediate threats.** Not every move must attack; improving your pieces counts.
- **Pawn structure matters.** The Exchange Variation teaches how a pawn majority can be turned into a passed pawn. See [king and pawn endgames](/chess/king-and-pawn-endgames/) for what happens when it does.
- **Be patient.** Many games are decided on move 25, not move 5.

If you prefer a simpler start, the [Italian Game](/chess/italian-game/) has similar ideas with less theory. Practise on [Chess 3D](https://chess3d.online).
