Strategy is the long-term plan behind the moves. Tactics win material; strategy decides which tactics are possible. These principles apply at every level.

## Opening principles

1. **Control the centre** with pawns on e4/d4 (or e5/d5).
2. **Develop** knights and bishops quickly, toward the centre.
3. **Castle** early: king safety first, rook activation second.
4. **Do not move the same piece twice** in the opening without a reason.
5. **Do not bring the queen out early**; she can be chased by minor pieces.

See [openings](/chess/openings/) for concrete lines.

## Middlegame ideas

- **Piece activity.** Put pieces where they control many squares. A knight in the centre beats a knight on the rim.
- **Open files for rooks.** Place rooks on open or half-open files.
- **Weak squares.** Target squares that cannot be defended by pawns.
- **Pawn structure.** Doubled and isolated pawns are weaknesses; passed pawns are strengths.
- **Improve your worst piece.** Find the piece doing the least and give it a job.
- **King safety.** Do not open lines near your king unless you are attacking.

## Trading pieces

| Situation | Preferred plan |
|---|---|
| Ahead in material | Trade pieces (not pawns) |
| Behind in material | Avoid trades; look for complications |
| Cramped | Trade to gain space |
| Attacking | Keep pieces; trade defenders |

## Beginner mistakes that cost the most games

1. **Hanging pieces** — moving a piece where it can be captured for free. Before every move ask "what does this leave undefended?".
2. **Ignoring the opponent's threats** — look at the last move: what does it attack?
3. **Early queen sorties** — they lose time.
4. **Too many pawn moves** — develop pieces first.
5. **Not castling** — the king in the centre becomes a target.
6. **Grabbing pawns when behind in development.**
7. **Playing too fast** — slow down in complicated positions.

## A practical improvement plan

- **Play** longer games (10+0 or 15+10) rather than only bullet.
- **Solve tactics** every day ([tactics guide](/chess/tactics/)).
- **Review** your games: find the first big mistake.
- **Learn** basic [endgames](/chess/endgames/).
- **Track your rating** with [ELO](/elo-rating-explained/) to see progress.

## Thinking process for each move

See also the [10 common beginner mistakes](/chess/beginner-mistakes/) and [piece values](/chess/piece-values/).

1. What did my opponent's last move do?
2. Are there checks, captures or threats for me?
3. Are any of my pieces loose?
4. What is my plan?
5. Before moving: does my move leave something hanging?
