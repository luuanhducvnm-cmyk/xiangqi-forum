The **Italian Game** begins **1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4**. White develops two pieces, points the bishop at f7 (the weakest square in Black's camp, defended only by the king) and prepares to castle. It is one of the oldest openings still played at every level. This page covers the ideas, not a complete repertoire. **Every position below was checked by playing the moves in the Chess 3D rules engine.**

## Black's main replies

### 3...Bc5, the Giuoco Piano

Black develops the bishop to the equal square. White's most principled plan is **c3 and d4** to build a big pawn centre: 4.c3 Nf6 5.d4 exd4 6.cxd4 Bb4+. Black checks and the game becomes sharp.

{{img:ln-giuoco|Giuoco Piano main line: 4.c3 Nf6 5.d4 exd4 6.cxd4 Bb4+.}}

A slower plan is 4.c3 and 5.d3, a patient setup often called the Giuoco Pianissimo ("very quiet game"). It keeps the tension and aims for a later d4 or a kingside plan.

White can also play **4.b4**, the **Evans Gambit**, offering a pawn to gain time for the centre.

{{img:ln-evans|Evans Gambit: 4.b4, a pawn offered for quick development and the centre.}}

### 3...Nf6, the Two Knights Defence

Black counterattacks the e4 pawn instead.

{{img:ln-twoknights|Two Knights Defence: 3...Nf6 attacks the e4 pawn.}}

White's most ambitious try is **4.Ng5**, attacking f7 a second time. Black's best-known answer is 4...d5 5.exd5. If Black recaptures with 5...Nxd5 White can play **6.Nxf7**, the **Fried Liver Attack**, a knight sacrifice that drags the black king into the open. It is risky for both sides, so Black players usually prefer 5...Na5, keeping the extra pawn.

{{img:ln-friedliver|Fried Liver Attack: 6.Nxf7, a sharp knight sacrifice after 5...Nxd5.}}

## Typical plans

- **White:** castle early, play c3 and d4, keep an eye on f7 and avoid pushing too many pawns.
- **Black:** develop bishop and knight, castle, and challenge the centre with ...d5 or ...exd4 at the right moment.
- **Both:** do not move the queen out early. The Italian punishes loose pieces with tactics like the [Légal trap](/chess/opening-traps/).

## Common beginner mistakes

1. Moving the same piece twice in the opening instead of developing new pieces.
2. Forgetting that f7 (or f2) is only defended by the king.
3. Sacrificing a piece for an attack without calculating the consequences. Practise these ideas with our [mate-in-2 puzzles](/chess/puzzles/mate-in-2/).
