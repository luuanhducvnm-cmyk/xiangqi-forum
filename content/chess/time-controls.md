A chess game is usually played against a clock. Time controls tell you how much time each player has, and they decide the character of the game.

## Reading a time control

Time controls are written as **minutes + seconds**:

- `10+0` — 10 minutes each, no increment.
- `10+5` — 10 minutes each plus 5 seconds added after each move.
- `3+2` — 3 minutes each plus 2 seconds per move.

## The four categories

The names below are the commonly used categories. Exact cut-offs differ between organisations; the FIDE definitions are based on the total time each player has.

| Name | Typical time per player | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| Bullet | Under 3 minutes (often 1+0 or 1+1) | Pure speed; premoves and instincts |
| Blitz | About 3 to 10 minutes (3+2, 5+0) | Fast and tactical |
| Rapid | About 10 to 60 minutes (10+5, 15+10) | Time to think, great for improving |
| Classical | More than 60 minutes | Deep strategic play |

**For learning, rapid is best.** You have time to check threats and to understand your mistakes afterwards.

## Increment and delay

- **Increment (Fischer):** after every move, extra seconds are added to your clock. If you play fast, your time can grow.
- **Delay (Bronstein / simple delay):** the clock waits a few seconds each turn before counting down. Unused delay time is not added.

Increment is the common online format and is used in [Chess 3D](https://chess3d.online).

## How a two-button chess clock works

A physical chess clock has two displays and two buttons — one for each player.

1. White starts the game; White's clock runs.
2. After moving, a player **presses their own button**. That stops their clock and starts their opponent's.
3. If a clock reaches zero, that player has lost on time (unless the opponent cannot possibly checkmate, in which case it is a draw).

Chess 3D copies this: you make your move, then press your side's big button on the 3D clock. If you prefer, turn on the **auto-clock** button in the top-left toolbar, and your move will end your turn automatically.

## Time management

- **Do not spend too long in the opening** — use your knowledge, play quickly.
- **Take time in critical positions** where a tactic or a plan is at stake.
- **Keep a minute or two** at the end; low time is when most blunders happen.
- **Increment helps**: in a 10+5 game you can often save time in simple positions.
