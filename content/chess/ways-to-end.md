A chess game can end in nine ways. Knowing them helps you avoid losing won games and save lost ones.

## Wins

### 1. Checkmate

The king is attacked and cannot escape. This is the normal way to win. See the [checkmate patterns](/chess/checkmates/).

### 2. Resignation

A player who knows the position is lost can resign. This is common even at master level. It is usually much more polite than playing on in a hopeless position.

### 3. Time

If a player's clock runs out, that player loses — unless the opponent has no possible way to checkmate (then it is a draw).

## Draws

### 4. Stalemate

The player to move is **not in check** but has **no legal move**. The game is a draw. It is a common trap when you are winning ([endgames](/chess/endgames/)).

{{img:ch-stalemate|Stalemate: Black to move has no legal move and is not in check — a draw.}}

### 5. Insufficient material

Neither player can checkmate by any legal sequence. Examples: king vs king, king and bishop vs king, king and knight vs king, and king and bishop vs king and bishop of the same colour squares.

### 6. Threefold repetition

If the same position appears three times, either player can claim a draw (online games usually apply it automatically).

### 7. The fifty-move rule

If 50 consecutive moves by each player pass with no pawn move and no capture, the game can be declared drawn.

### 8. Draw by agreement

Players may agree to a draw: one offers, the other accepts. In tournament chess, a draw offer is made after your own move. In rated online games, draw by agreement is often available after a few moves; in [Chess 3D](https://chess3d.online) a draw by agreement ends the game without changing either rating.

### 9. Perpetual check

One player can force a draw by giving check every move so that the position repeats. It is a way to save a lost game.

## Tips

- **Before offering a draw**, check whether you are better — if you are, play on.
- **Avoid stalemate** when you are winning: always check whether the opponent has a legal move.
- **If you are losing**, look for perpetual check and stalemate resources.
- **Watch the clock** — many games are lost on time rather than on the board ([time controls](/chess/time-controls/)).
