At the start of your chess journey, an opening should do three simple things: **develop your pieces, control the centre and get your king safe**. You do not need long theory. The openings below are popular with beginners because their ideas are easy to follow. The page [chess openings](/chess/openings/) covers more of them.

## The principles first

1. **Control the centre** with pawns on e4/d4 (or e5/d5) and with your pieces.
2. **Develop knights and bishops** before moving other pieces.
3. **Castle early** to protect your king ([special moves](/chess/special-moves/)).
4. **Avoid moving the same piece twice** or bringing the queen out early ([beginner mistakes](/chess/beginner-mistakes/)).

## For White

### Italian Game: 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4

A classic open game. White points the bishop at the weak f7 square and prepares to castle.

{{img:ch-italian|Italian Game after 3.Bc4: knights and bishop developed, ready to castle.}}

**Why it suits beginners:** every move follows the principles, and the plans (castle, play d3/c3, open the centre) are easy to remember.

### London System: 1.d4 d5 2.Bf4

A solid setup White can play against many replies, with the bishop developed outside the pawn chain.

{{img:ch-london|London System after 2.Bf4.}}

**Why it suits beginners:** you can follow the same setup in most games, so there is little to memorise. The drawback is that you learn less about open positions.

### Queen's Gambit: 1.d4 d5 2.c4

White offers a pawn to take control of the centre. If Black accepts, White usually regains it.

{{img:ch-queens-gambit|Queen's Gambit: 1.d4 d5 2.c4.}}

**Why it suits beginners:** it teaches central play and piece activity, and the name "gambit" is misleading because Black cannot easily keep the pawn.

## For Black

### Reply to 1.e4 with 1...e5

This mirrors White's move, claims the centre and leads to open games such as the Italian Game. It is the most direct way to follow the principles.

### Caro-Kann Defence: 1.e4 c6

A solid reply that prepares ...d5 with a sound pawn structure and few early weaknesses.

{{img:ch-caro-kann|Caro-Kann Defence: 1.e4 c6.}}

**Why it suits beginners:** the king stays safe and the ideas are clear. Black may play more passively than in open games.

### Against 1.d4

Reply 1...d5 to take the centre, and develop your knights and bishops. The Queen's Gambit and London System are common here.

## What to avoid as a beginner

- **Long memorised lines** you do not understand. As soon as the opponent leaves the book, you will be lost.
- **Sharp gambits** that sacrifice material without understanding the follow-up.
- **Changing your opening every game.** Play the same two openings for several weeks, so you learn what typically happens.

## How to practise

1. Choose one opening for White and one for Black.
2. Play ten games with each and write down where you went wrong.
3. Read the [strategy guide](/chess/strategy/) and the [tactics](/chess/tactics/) that arise in your games.
