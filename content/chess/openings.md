An opening is the first phase of the game, when you develop your pieces. You do not need to memorise much, but knowing a few systems — with their ideas — saves time and avoids traps. Moves are written in [algebraic notation](/chess/notation/).

## Open games (1.e4 e5)

### Italian Game — 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4

White develops quickly and aims the bishop at the weak f7 square. A friendly, classical opening that teaches good habits.

{{img:ch-italian|Italian Game after 3.Bc4.}}

### Ruy Lopez — 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5

The "Spanish Game". White pressures the knight that defends e5. It is one of the oldest and most respected openings, leading to rich strategic play.

{{img:ch-ruy-lopez|Ruy Lopez after 3.Bb5.}}

## Semi-open games (1.e4 other)

### Sicilian Defence — 1.e4 c5

Black fights for the centre with a flank pawn and creates an unbalanced game. It is the most popular reply to 1.e4 at all levels, but also the most complicated.

{{img:ch-sicilian|Sicilian Defence: 1.e4 c5.}}

### French Defence — 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5

A solid but slightly cramped setup: Black builds a pawn chain and counterattacks the centre.

{{img:ch-french|French Defence: 1.e4 e6.}}

### Caro-Kann Defence — 1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5

Like the French, but the light-squared bishop is not locked in. Very solid and simple to learn.

{{img:ch-caro-kann|Caro-Kann Defence: 1.e4 c6.}}

## Closed games (1.d4)

### Queen's Gambit — 1.d4 d5 2.c4

White offers a pawn to deflect Black's centre. Black can usually not keep the pawn safely, and White gets an active game.

{{img:ch-queens-gambit|Queen's Gambit: 1.d4 d5 2.c4.}}

### London System — 1.d4 d5 2.Bf4

A solid system where White develops with Bf4, e3, Nf3 and c3 regardless of what Black does. Very easy to learn.

{{img:ch-london|London System: 1.d4 d5 2.Bf4.}}

### King's Indian Defence — 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6

Black lets White take the centre, then attacks it with pieces and pawns. A fighting opening for players who like counter-attacks.

{{img:ch-kings-indian|King's Indian Defence setup: 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6.}}

## Principles for any opening

1. **Control the centre** with pawns (e4/d4 or e5/d5) and pieces.
2. **Develop knights and bishops** before moving the queen or rooks.
3. **Castle early** to put the king in safety.
4. **Do not move the same piece twice** without a reason.
5. **Do not capture just because you can**; check what is protected.
6. **Know common traps**, like [scholar's mate](/chess/checkmates/).

## Practise

Learn one opening as White and one as Black, then play it repeatedly in [Chess 3D](https://chess3d.online) until you understand its typical plans. Read about [strategy](/chess/strategy/) afterwards.
