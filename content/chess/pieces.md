A chess piece moves according to its own rule, and captures the same way it moves — except pawns. Each diagram below uses the game's rules engine: green dots are legal moves, red rings are captures.

## King (♔ ♚)

The king moves **one square in any direction**. It cannot move into check. Protect it: castle early ([special moves](/chess/special-moves/)).

{{img:ch-king|A king on e4 can move to eight squares.}}

## Queen (♕ ♛)

The most powerful piece. She moves **any distance along a rank, file or diagonal**. Her value is about 9 pawns. Do not bring her out too early.

{{img:ch-queen|The queen on d4 attacks along ranks, files and diagonals and can capture the pawn on d5.}}

## Rook (♖ ♜)

The rook moves **any distance along a rank or file**. It is worth about 5 pawns. Rooks work best on open files (files with no pawns).

{{img:ch-rook|The rook on d4 can go along its rank and file; it can capture the pawn on g4.}}

## Bishop (♗ ♝)

The bishop moves **any distance diagonally**. A bishop stays on one colour of squares for the whole game. It is worth about 3 pawns.

{{img:ch-bishop|A bishop on d4 slides along its diagonals.}}

## Knight (♘ ♞)

The knight moves in an **L-shape**: two squares one way, one square perpendicular. It is the only piece that can **jump over** other pieces. It is worth about 3 pawns, and is strongest in the centre.

{{img:ch-knight|A knight on d4 has eight possible squares.}}

## Pawn (♙ ♟)

Pawns move **forward one square** (or **two squares** from the starting rank), and capture **one square diagonally forward**. They never move backward. A pawn that reaches the last rank is promoted (see [special moves](/chess/special-moves/)).

{{img:ch-pawn-start|The pawn on e2 can step to e3, jump to e4 or capture the pawn on d3.}}

## Piece values (rough guide)

| Piece | Value |
|---|---|
| Pawn | 1 |
| Knight | 3 |
| Bishop | 3 |
| Rook | 5 |
| Queen | 9 |
| King | priceless |

Values help you decide whether a trade is fair. For example, trading a bishop for a rook (3 for 5) loses material. But activity and position can be worth more than raw points — see [strategy](/chess/strategy/).

## Tips for using the pieces

- **Control the centre** with pawns and knights.
- **Develop minor pieces** (knights and bishops) before rooks and queen.
- **Put rooks on open files** and use them together.
- **Keep your king safe**, and do not expose it early.
- **Count attackers and defenders** before you capture.
