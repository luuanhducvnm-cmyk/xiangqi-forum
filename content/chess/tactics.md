A **tactic** is a short sequence of forcing moves that wins material or checkmates. Most games below master level are decided by tactics. Learn these patterns and look for them on every move.

## Always look for: checks, captures, threats

Before each move check, in this order: *Checks* (can I or my opponent give check?), *Captures* (can anything be captured?), *Threats* (what does each move attack?). This simple habit catches most blunders.

## Fork

A move that attacks two or more pieces at once. The opponent can only save one.

{{img:ch-fork|Knight fork: Nc7+ attacks the king on e8 and the rook on a8.}}

Knights are the great forkers, but pawns, queens and bishops fork too. A **royal fork** attacks the king and a major piece.

## Pin

A piece is **pinned** when it cannot move without exposing a more valuable piece behind it.

{{img:ch-pin|The knight on c6 is pinned to the king by the bishop on b5. It has no legal moves.}}

- An **absolute pin** is against the king: the pinned piece cannot legally move.
- A **relative pin** is against a lesser piece: moving is legal but loses material.

Attack pinned pieces — they cannot defend well.

## Skewer

The reverse of a pin: a valuable piece is attacked and must move, exposing a piece behind it. Rooks, bishops and queens skewer along lines.

## Discovered attack

Move one piece away to reveal an attack by another piece behind it. A **discovered check** is especially strong because the moving piece can do something else while the opponent must deal with the check. A **double check** (two pieces give check at once) can only be answered by a king move.

## Removing the defender and deflection

- **Removing the defender**: capture or attack the piece that protects the target.
- **Deflection**: force a defender away from its duty.
- **Decoy**: lure a piece to a bad square.

## Back-rank tactics

If the king is trapped on its back rank behind its own pawns, a rook or queen check there is checkmate ([back-rank mate](/chess/checkmates/)). Give your king "luft" — a pawn move to make an escape square — to avoid it.

## Zwischenzug

An "in-between move": instead of making the expected recapture, you insert a check or a stronger threat first, then recapture. Always ask "do I have to recapture right now?".

## How to practise

- Solve tactics puzzles for 10–15 minutes a day.
- After each game, find missed tactics in the moves.
- Play slower games where you have time to check for tactics.
- Play against the Royal AI in [Chess 3D](https://chess3d.online) to practise spotting threats.
