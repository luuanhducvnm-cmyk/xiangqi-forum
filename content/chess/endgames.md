The endgame is the last phase of the game, when few pieces remain. Many games are decided here, and a small set of basic positions gives you a huge advantage.

## Activate the king

In the middlegame the king hides. In the endgame he becomes a fighting piece: bring him to the centre and use him to support pawns.

## King and queen vs king

This is a win; the technique is:

1. Use the queen to **restrict** the enemy king, one square away in a knight's move.
2. Bring your king closer.
3. Force the enemy king to the edge, then deliver mate ([final position](/chess/checkmates/)).
4. **Watch for stalemate**: never leave the opponent with no legal moves when not in check.

{{img:ch-stalemate|Careful: here Black is not in check but has no legal move — stalemate, a draw. Do not do this when you are winning!}}

## King and rook vs king

Also a win, but it takes more moves. Use your king and rook together to push the enemy king to the edge. Methods ("the box method") exist and are worth learning; the main principle is to use the rook to cut the king off along a rank or file while your king approaches.

## Which endgames are drawn?

| Material | Result |
|---|---|
| King vs king | Draw (insufficient material) |
| King + bishop vs king | Draw |
| King + knight vs king | Draw |
| King + two knights vs king | Mate cannot be forced; effectively a draw |
| King + bishop + bishop vs king | Win |
| King + bishop + knight vs king | Win (a difficult technique) |
| King + rook vs king | Win |
| King + queen vs king | Win |

## The opposition

The kings stand with one square between them. The player **not** to move has the opposition and can force the other king to give way.

{{img:ch-opp|The kings face each other. With White to move, Black holds the opposition.}}

Opposition decides many king-and-pawn endings: the side that holds the opposition can win the key squares in front of the pawn.

## The rule of the square

To see whether a king can catch a passed pawn, draw a square from the pawn to its promotion square. If the king can step into the square (it is the king's move), he catches the pawn.

## Rook endings

Rook endings are the most common. Rules of thumb: **keep your rook active, put it behind passed pawns, and cut the enemy king off.** Famous positions (Lucena and Philidor) show how to win or draw.

See also [stalemate](/chess/stalemate/), the most common way to waste a winning endgame.

## Practise

Play simple endings against the Royal AI in [Chess 3D](https://chess3d.online), and remember: before every move in a winning endgame, check for **stalemate**.
