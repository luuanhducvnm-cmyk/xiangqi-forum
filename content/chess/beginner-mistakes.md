Almost every chess player makes the same mistakes when starting out. The good news is that each one has a simple fix, and fixing them is the fastest way to win more games.

## 1. Leaving pieces undefended

A "hanging" piece is one that can be captured for free. **Fix:** before every move, ask what your move leaves unprotected, and what your opponent can now capture.

## 2. Ignoring your opponent's last move

Beginners often play their own plan and miss the threat their opponent just created. **Fix:** after each reply, ask "what does that move attack, and what does it threaten next?"

## 3. Bringing the queen out too early

The queen is strong but valuable, so enemy pieces can chase it and win tempo. **Fix:** develop knights and bishops first, and keep the queen back until the position is open.

## 4. Moving the same piece twice in the opening

Every move in the opening should bring a new piece into play. **Fix:** make one move per piece until your knights and bishops are developed.

## 5. Too many pawn moves

Pawn moves cannot be taken back and do not develop pieces. **Fix:** move the centre pawns for space, then spend your moves on pieces.

## 6. Not castling

A king in the centre can be attacked by many pieces. **Fix:** castle early (see [special moves](/chess/special-moves/)), usually within the first ten moves.

## 7. Grabbing pawns when behind in development

Taking a free pawn can cost several tempos and lead to a strong attack against you. **Fix:** finish developing, then decide whether the pawn is really free.

## 8. Trading pieces without a plan

Trades change the balance of the game. **Fix:** use the [piece values](/chess/piece-values/) to check whether a trade is fair, and ask whether it helps your king safety or opens lines for your attack ([strategy](/chess/strategy/)).

## 9. Moving too fast

Most blunders come from quick moves. **Fix:** in positions with captures or checks, slow down and look at each one. Longer time controls, such as 10+5, give you time to think.

## 10. Never reviewing your games

If you do not find your mistakes, you repeat them. **Fix:** after each game, find the move where the evaluation changed and ask what you missed.

## A simple check before every move

1. **Checks:** can my opponent give check? Can I?
2. **Captures:** can anything be captured on either side?
3. **Threats:** what is my opponent threatening now, and after my move?
4. **Safety:** is every piece I own defended, and is my king safe?

This takes ten seconds and prevents most beginner blunders. The [tactics guide](/chess/tactics/) shows the patterns to look for, such as forks and pins.
