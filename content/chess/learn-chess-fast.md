You do not need months of study to start enjoying chess. This plan takes about 30 to 45 minutes a day for one week and takes you from the rules to your first games against real opponents. It is a plan, not a promise: how fast you improve depends on how much you play and how well you review your games.

## Day 1: the board and the pieces (30 minutes)

Read [how to play chess](/chess/how-to-play/) and set up the board: white square in the bottom-right corner, queen on her own colour. Then study how each piece moves on the [pieces page](/chess/pieces/). Do not worry about strategy yet.

## Day 2: the full rules (40 minutes)

Read the [rules](/chess/rules/) and the [special moves](/chess/special-moves/): castling, en passant and promotion. Then play one game against the computer just to practise moves. It does not matter if you lose.

## Day 3: how a game ends (30 minutes)

Read [checkmate patterns](/chess/checkmates/), [ways a game can end](/chess/ways-to-end/) and [stalemate](/chess/stalemate/). Stalemate and checkmate are easy to confuse, and knowing the difference can win or save a game.

## Day 4: piece values and basic strategy (40 minutes)

Read the [piece values](/chess/piece-values/) guide and [beginner strategy](/chess/strategy/). Ideas to take away: control the centre, develop knights and bishops, castle early, and do not trade without a reason. Play two games and try to apply them.

## Day 5: tactics (45 minutes)

Read the [tactics page](/chess/tactics/): forks, pins, skewers and discovered attacks. After each game, find one moment where you or your opponent missed a tactic. This habit improves results faster than any opening theory.

## Day 6: one opening for each colour (40 minutes)

Pick one simple setup and learn only the first five moves of it. The [best openings for beginners](/chess/best-openings-for-beginners/) page suggests a few. Use the [notation guide](/chess/notation/) to read moves. Play two games with the opening and see how your opponent replies.

## Day 7: avoid the classic mistakes (45 minutes)

Read the [10 common beginner mistakes](/chess/beginner-mistakes/) and use the four-step check before every move: checks, captures, threats, safety. Then play your first games against real opponents. Do not worry about your rating: the first games are for learning.

## After the first week

- **Play regularly.** Ten minutes a day beats three hours on one day. A [10+5 game](/chess/time-controls/) is a good length for learning.
- **Review your losses.** Find the move where the game turned and ask what you missed.
- **Learn endgames next.** Read the [endgame principles](/chess/endgames/), then add a second opening.
- **Use the glossary** when a term confuses you ([glossary](/chess/glossary/)).
