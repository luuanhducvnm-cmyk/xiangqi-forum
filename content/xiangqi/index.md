Xiangqi (象棋), usually called **Chinese chess** in English, is a two-player strategy game in which each side tries to checkmate the opponent's general. If you already play chess, you will recognise the idea at once — and enjoy the differences. If you have never played either, xiangqi is a perfectly good place to start.

This section is a complete, beginner-friendly course. Every diagram is generated from the rules engine used by [Xiangqi 3D](https://xiangqi3d.online), so the positions and move dots are guaranteed to be legal.

## Xiangqi at a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Players | 2 (Red moves first, Black second) |
| Board | 9 files × 10 ranks; pieces stand on the intersections |
| Pieces per side | 16: 1 general, 2 advisors, 2 elephants, 2 horses, 2 chariots, 2 cannons, 5 soldiers |
| Goal | Checkmate — or leave the opponent with no legal move |
| Unique features | The river, the two palaces, the cannon's jump capture and the flying-general rule |
| Special chess rules | None: no castling, no en passant, no promotion |
