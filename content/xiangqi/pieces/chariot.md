The **chariot** (Red 俥, Black 車; read *jū* in xiangqi, *chē* in everyday Chinese) is the most powerful piece on the board.

## How it moves

A chariot slides **any distance along a rank or a file**. It stops in front of a friendly piece, or captures the first enemy piece in its path. It cannot jump over pieces.

{{img:xq-chariot|The chariot can move along its rank and file and capture the Black soldier on its rank; its own soldier blocks the file above.}}

It captures the same way it moves, and it is unaffected by the river — it can cross freely.

## Why it is so strong

- It controls up to **17 points** at once from an open board (9 along a rank + 10 along a file − 2 overlapping), more than any other piece.
- It cannot be blocked like the horse or elephant.
- It is the main attacker in almost every winning combination.

For this reason the standard strategic plan is: **get the chariots out early** and fight for open files.

## How to use it

1. **Open a path.** In the opening, a chariot begins in the corner behind a horse and a soldier. Move the horse (and sometimes the soldier or cannon) to unblock it.
2. **Take the open file.** A chariot on a file with no pieces in front of it pins and pressures anything on that file.
3. **Go to the river.** A chariot on the second rank from the river controls the whole rank and eyes the enemy flank.
4. **Back it up.** An unprotected chariot invites a trade for a lesser piece. Keep it protected, and avoid exchanging it for a horse or cannon unless there is a clear gain.

## Attacking the general

A chariot on the opponent's back rank (the "bottom line") together with another chariot or a horse gives some of the quickest mates: see [double-chariot and chariot + horse mates](/xiangqi/checkmate-patterns/).

## Common mistakes

- Leaving a chariot in the corner for twenty moves.
- Trading a chariot for a cannon and a soldier in a position where the chariot would have dominated the open board.
- Placing a chariot where an enemy horse can fork it with the general.

## Value

About **9 points**, roughly nine soldiers. In material counting, losing a chariot for a horse or cannon is a big mistake.
