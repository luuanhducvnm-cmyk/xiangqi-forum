The **cannon** (Red 炮, Black 砲, *pào*) is the piece that makes xiangqi unique.

## How it moves and captures

**Moving:** like a chariot — any distance along a rank or file through empty points, no jumping.

**Capturing:** different from moving. A cannon captures by **jumping over exactly one piece** — the **screen** (also called the "platform") — in a straight line, landing on the next piece beyond it, which it captures. The screen may belong to either player. If there are no pieces, or two or more between the cannon and its target, there is no capture.

{{img:xq-cannon|Cannon on the left. Rightwards it jumps its own soldier (the screen) to capture the Black soldier; upwards the Black soldier is a screen, so it can capture the Black horse behind it.}}

Read the diagram carefully:

- The cannon can **move** (not capture) to any empty point along its rank and file up to the first piece.
- It **cannot** capture the first piece it meets: with no screen in front of it there is nothing to jump.
- Once a screen is in place, it **captures** the first piece behind the screen (dashed rings).

## Screens are everything

The cannon's power depends on screens. In the opening, the cannons are the main attackers: a cannon on the central file aims straight at the enemy general, and the pieces in between become screens. For this reason the *central cannon* opening ([C2=5](/xiangqi/openings/)) is the most popular move in xiangqi.

## What the cannon is good at

- **Threats along files and ranks** through a screen.
- **Pinning** an enemy piece against a more valuable piece behind it.
- **Check through a screen**: a cannon on the general's file with one screen between can give check.
- **Supporting a chariot** in attacks near the palace ([checkmate patterns](/xiangqi/checkmate-patterns/)).

## Weaknesses

- It needs a screen to capture, so it is weaker when the board is empty.
- It can be attacked by any piece; a cannon is worth about 4½, but it is easy to trap on an edge.
- It cannot capture an adjacent piece without a screen.

## Value

About **4½ points**: more than a horse in a crowded position, less in an open one. In the opening and middlegame many players prefer to keep their cannons and trade horses; in endgames the reverse is often true.
