The **horse** (Red 傌, Black 馬, *mǎ*) is the xiangqi cousin of the chess knight — with an important difference: it can be blocked.

## How it moves

The horse moves in two steps:

1. One point **straight** along a rank or file (this is its "leg").
2. Then one point **diagonally outward**, away from where it started.

The leg point **must be empty**. If any piece (friend or enemy) stands there, the horse cannot make either move that goes through that leg.

## Hobbling the horse's leg

Because the leg point must be empty, a horse next to other pieces loses moves quickly. In the diagram below the horse's own pawns on the point above it and to its right block four of its eight jumps.

{{img:xq-horse-block|Two blocked legs cost the horse four moves. This is called "hobbling the horse's leg" (蹩馬腿).}}

Lessons:

- **Keep your horses unblocked** — do not stack soldiers and pieces on their legs.
- **Block the opponent's horse** with a cheap piece — a soldier can neutralise a valuable horse.
- **Attack a horse from its blind side:** a blocked horse cannot capture the piece that is attacking it.

## What the horse does best

- **Forks.** Landing on a point that attacks two pieces at once is the horse's speciality ([tactics](/xiangqi/tactics/)).
- **Mating in the palace.** A horse deep in the enemy palace, supported by a chariot or cannon, delivers some of the most elegant checkmates ([checkmate patterns](/xiangqi/checkmate-patterns/)).
- **Controlling the centre** early in the game, which is why the standard development moves bring both horses forward.

## Weaknesses

- Slow: it takes several moves to get across the board.
- On the edge it has few legal moves; a horse in a corner is almost useless.
- It can be hobbled — see above.

## Value

About **4 points** (a soldier is 1, a chariot about 9). In the opening and middlegame a horse is roughly equal to a cannon; in endgames with few pieces the horse tends to gain value because it cannot be hindered by a missing screen.
