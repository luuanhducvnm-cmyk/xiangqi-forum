Each side starts with 16 pieces of seven kinds. Their names are the same for both players; only the characters differ between Red and Black. Click any piece below for a full page with move diagrams.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| [Elephant](/xiangqi/pieces/elephant/) | 相 / 象 | xiàng | 2 | ≈ 2 |
| [Horse](/xiangqi/pieces/horse/) | 傌 / 馬 | mǎ | 2 | ≈ 4 |

| [Soldier](/xiangqi/pieces/soldier/) | 兵 / 卒 | bīng / zú | 5 | 1 (more after the river) |

The values are rule-of-thumb numbers — they help you judge trades, but position matters more than arithmetic. A soldier beyond the river, or a cannon with the right screens, can be worth much more than its listed value.

## Attackers and defenders

Xiangqi pieces fall into two natural groups:

- **Attackers**: the chariot, the cannon, the horse and the soldiers. These win games.
- **Defenders**: the advisors and the elephants. They cannot cross the river (elephants) or leave the palace (advisors), but together they form a wall that is very hard to break through.

The **general** is in a class of its own: it never moves far, but its position decides whether a given attack works, and in the endgame it also controls its file.

## The big surprises for chess players

1. **The cannon.** It moves like a chariot but captures by jumping over exactly one piece. No chess piece does that.
2. **Blocked moves.** The horse and the elephant can be blocked by a piece next to or between them — chess knights cannot.
3. **Confined pieces.** The general, advisors and elephants have limited zones.
4. **Soldiers.** They move one step and never retreat, but gain sideways movement after crossing the river. They never promote.

## How the pieces work together

- **Chariot + cannon** is the classic attacking pair: the chariot gives check, the cannon covers an escape or supplies the screen.
- **Horse + cannon** forks and mates are the core tactical patterns — see [checkmate patterns](/xiangqi/checkmate-patterns/).
- **Advisors + elephants** make a defensive wall around the general.
