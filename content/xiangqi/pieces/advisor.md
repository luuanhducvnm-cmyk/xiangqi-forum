The **advisor** (Red 仕, Black 士; sometimes called "guard" or "mandarin") is the general's bodyguard.

## How it moves

An advisor moves **one point diagonally**, and it must stay in the **palace**. Because the palace has only five points on its diagonals (the four corners and the centre), an advisor always stands on one of those five.

{{img:xq-advisor|An advisor on the palace centre can step to any of the four corners.}}

Advisors capture the same way they move: diagonally onto an enemy piece inside the palace.

## What advisors do

- **Protect the general** against checks from horses and cannons by covering escape squares and capturing intruders.
- **Cover the corners from the centre.** An advisor on the palace centre point guards all four diagonal points at once, so it is often the most flexible spot for it.
- **Take intruders.** An enemy horse or soldier that steps onto a palace diagonal point can often be captured by an advisor.

## When to move them

- At the start, leave them alone. Developing a chariot or horse is almost always better.
- Move one to the centre when you need to cover several palace points at once. Remember that any piece standing on the general's file can act as a screen for an enemy cannon, so check the file first.
- Do not trade advisors lightly: once both are gone, the general is exposed to chariots and cannons.

## Value

An advisor is worth about **2 soldiers**. In the endgame, advisors plus elephants can often hold a draw against a lone attacker, which is why trading them away is a strategic decision.
