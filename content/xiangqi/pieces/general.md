The **general** (Red 帥, Black 將, called *shuài* and *jiàng*) is the piece you must protect. Lose it — be checkmated — and the game is over.

## How the general moves

The general moves **one point at a time, up, down, left or right**, and may never leave its **palace**, the 3×3 zone marked with diagonals. It never moves diagonally.

{{img:xq-king|From the centre of the palace the general can reach four points.}}

A general in a palace corner has only two moves; on an edge point three. This lack of room is why back-rank checkmates are so common.

## Capturing

The general captures like it moves: onto an adjacent palace point occupied by an enemy piece, provided that point is not protected. Beginners often forget this when an enemy piece lands next to their general — sometimes it can simply be taken.

## The flying-general rule

The generals may **never face each other** with no piece between them on the same file. If a move would create that, it is illegal. A single piece between them is therefore pinned: it can slide along the file but must not leave it.

{{img:xq-flying|The Red chariot cannot leave the file: if it did, the generals would face each other.}}

This also gives the general an attacking role in endgames: your general can "attack" the file, and the opponent's general must step off it.

## Practical advice

- **Keep the advisors home** until you have a reason to move them; they are the general's bodyguards ([advisor page](/xiangqi/pieces/advisor/)).
- **Watch the file.** Before moving a piece that stands between the generals, check the flying-general rule.
- **Do not wander.** The general sits in the palace for a reason; an exposed palace invites horse and cannon mates ([checkmate patterns](/xiangqi/checkmate-patterns/)).
- **In the endgame** the general can step forward to the second or third rank of the palace to guard the file against chariots.
