The **elephant** (Red 相, Black 象; also "minister" or "bishop") is the second defender. In some sets Red's character is 相 (xiàng, "minister") and Black's is 象 (xiàng, "elephant") — both are the same piece.

## How it moves

The elephant moves **exactly two points diagonally**. It cannot jump: the point in between, called the **elephant eye** (象眼), must be empty. And it can never cross the river, so it has just **seven** points on its half of the board.

{{img:xq-elephant|The elephant can reach three points; the fourth is blocked because a piece stands on the elephant eye.}}

The black soldier in the diagram sits on the elephant eye for the upper-right move, so that move is not allowed.

## What elephants do

- **Defend the centre and the flanks.** They protect soldiers and pieces on their half.
- **Defend against horses and cannons.** A well-placed pair of elephants covers important points on the second and third ranks.
- **Hold endgame fortresses.** Together with advisors, elephants often make a position nearly impossible to break down.

## Weaknesses

- **Blocking the eye.** An enemy piece (or even one of your own) on the eye freezes the elephant.
- **Predictable.** A single move is easy for an attacker to plan around; a lone elephant is a target.
- **Chariots on the back rank** can pin an elephant to the general.

## Value

About **2 soldiers**, similar to an advisor. Trade it away only for a concrete gain — advisors and elephants are what make the palace solid.
