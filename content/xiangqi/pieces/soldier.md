The **soldier** (Red 兵, Black 卒, *bīng* and *zú*) is the humblest piece — and the one that decides many endgames.

## How it moves

- **Before crossing the river:** one point **forward** only.
- **After crossing the river:** one point forward **or one point sideways**.
- **Never backward.**

{{img:xq-soldier-before|A soldier that has not crossed the river has only one move: forward.}}

{{img:xq-soldier-after|Once across the river, the soldier gains the two sideways moves.}}

Soldiers capture the way they move: onto an enemy piece one point forward (or sideways if across the river). Unlike chess pawns, they have no special first move, no *en passant* and no promotion.

## Strategy

- **Soldiers cannot retreat**, so advancing one is a decision you cannot take back. Do not push soldiers without a purpose.
- **Central soldiers** (file 5) are valuable because they restrict the opponent's horses and elephants.
- **Crossing the river** at the right moment makes a soldier much more dangerous — it now attacks sideways and can penetrate to the palace.
- **A soldier next to the enemy palace** (on the third or fourth rank from the enemy back rank) is a powerful attacker: it can take advisors, and supports chariots and horses in mates.
- **Soldiers at the edge of the board** have limited room and are often lost to simple attacks.

## Soldiers in the endgame

When few pieces remain, soldiers matter more than their points suggest:

- A soldier that has crossed the river can drive an enemy general off its file.
- A pair of connected soldiers next to the palace often decides an ending.
- A soldier on the last rank can only move sideways — it is not useless, but it is awkward.

See [endgame principles](/xiangqi/endgames/) for how to use them.

## Value

**1 point** before crossing the river; after crossing the river many players count about **2**, and more near the palace. Even so, being a soldier down in the opening is minor compared with losing the initiative.
