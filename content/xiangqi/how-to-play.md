Xiangqi is easier to learn than its reputation suggests. In this guide you will learn the goal, the board, the seven kinds of piece, and how a game flows — enough to sit down and play a complete legal game.

## The goal

Each side has one **general** (帥 for Red, 將 for Black). You win by putting the enemy general in a position where it is attacked (**check**) and cannot escape (**checkmate**). Unlike most games, you never capture the general: the game ends one move before that would happen.

## The board

The board has **9 vertical lines (files)** and **10 horizontal lines (ranks)**. Pieces stand on the **intersections**, not inside squares, which makes 90 playing points.

{{img:xq-start|The starting position. Red (bottom) moves first.}}

Three features matter right away:

- **The river** — the empty band in the middle. It separates the two armies and changes how some pieces move.
- **The palaces** — the two 3×3 zones marked with diagonal lines. The general and the advisors can never leave their own palace.
- **Colours** — Red and Black. Red moves first.

Details are on the [board and setup page](/xiangqi/board-setup/).

## How the pieces move

| Piece | Moves | Quick memory hook |
|---|---|---|
| [General](/xiangqi/pieces/general/) 帥/將 | 1 step up, down, left or right — inside the palace | The king you must protect |
| [Advisor](/xiangqi/pieces/advisor/) 仕/士 | 1 step diagonally — inside the palace | The general's bodyguard |
| [Elephant](/xiangqi/pieces/elephant/) 相/象 | Exactly 2 points diagonally; cannot cross the river; blocked if the middle point is occupied | A careful defender |
| [Horse](/xiangqi/pieces/horse/) 傌/馬 | 1 step straight, then 1 step diagonally outward; blocked if the straight step is occupied | A knight that can be hobbled |
| [Chariot](/xiangqi/pieces/chariot/) 俥/車 | Any distance along a rank or file | The strongest piece — like a rook |
| [Cannon](/xiangqi/pieces/cannon/) 炮/砲 | Moves like a chariot; captures only by jumping over exactly one piece | Needs a "screen" to capture |
| [Soldier](/xiangqi/pieces/soldier/) 兵/卒 | 1 step forward; after crossing the river, also 1 step sideways | Never moves backward |

## Capturing

You capture by moving a piece onto a point occupied by an enemy piece, which is removed from the board. Every piece captures the same way it moves — except the cannon, which moves one way and captures another (see [the cannon page](/xiangqi/pieces/cannon/)).

## Check, checkmate and the flying general

When a general is attacked it is in **check**, and its owner must immediately respond by capturing the attacker, blocking the line of attack or moving the general. If none of those is possible, it is **checkmate** and the game is over.

Xiangqi has one special rule: the two generals may **never face each other** on an open file with nothing between them. That is called the "flying general" rule, and it creates pins and surprising mates — see the [complete rules](/xiangqi/rules/).

## A first game, in order

1. **Red moves first.** A very natural first move is the central cannon: the cannon on file 2 slides to the middle file (written `C2=5`, see [notation](/xiangqi/notation/)).
2. **Develop your big pieces.** Get your chariots out of the corners, bring out the horses, and use the cannons with a screen in front of them. [Beginner strategy](/xiangqi/strategy/) explains why.
3. **Keep your general safe.** Advisors and elephants rarely attack, but they are what makes your palace hard to crack.
4. **Look for forks and double attacks.** The horse and the cannon are the main tactical pieces ([tactics](/xiangqi/tactics/)).
5. **Aim for a mating net.** Most beginner games end with a chariot plus one helper — see [checkmate patterns](/xiangqi/checkmate-patterns/).

## How a game can end

- **Checkmate** — the general is attacked and cannot escape.
- **No legal move** — if you have pieces but none of them can legally move, you lose.
- **Resignation** or **running out of time** on the clock.
- **Draw** — by agreement, by repetition, or after a long run without captures. Exact draw rules differ between rule sets; see [rules](/xiangqi/rules/).

## Common beginner mistakes

- Forgetting that the horse can be blocked, or that the elephant "eye" can be blocked.
- Moving a cannon to a spot where it has no screen and no job.
- Leaving both chariots at home in the corners for too long.
- Moving a soldier forward without a plan: soldiers cannot go back.
- Exposing the general by letting the files line up — the flying-general rule can lose games instantly.

## Practise on a real 3D board
