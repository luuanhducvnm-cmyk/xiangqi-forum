If you come from chess, this is the rule that surprises you most. In chess, a player who has no legal move but is not in check has been **stalemated**, and the game is a **draw**. In xiangqi the same situation is a **loss** for the player who cannot move.

## The rule in one sentence

If it is your turn and you have no legal move, you lose. It does not matter whether your general is in check: no legal move is enough. (When the general is in check as well, it is simply checkmate.)

In chess this would be a draw. In xiangqi, Black has lost.

## Why the rule matters

- **Attackers are rewarded for trapping.** You do not have to deliver check: taking away every legal move wins the game, so squeezing the opponent is a real plan.
- **Defenders must keep a free move.** A defending side that locks its own pieces in the corner, or whose only mobile pieces get traded off, can lose without ever being in check.
- **Endgames are more decisive.** Chess endgames are often drawn by stalemate tricks. In xiangqi the equivalent tricks do not save the weaker side, which is one reason xiangqi endgames produce fewer draws.

## How it differs from perpetual check and draws

Stalemate is separate from the draw rules. Draws still exist in xiangqi (by agreement, repetition under certain conditions, and long sequences without captures) and are covered in the [complete rules](/xiangqi/rules/), but "no legal move" is never one of them.

## Practical tips

1. **Count the opponent's moves.** In an endgame ask how many legal moves the opposing side actually has. If it is very few, look for a quiet move that removes the last one.
2. **Do not hobble your own pieces.** A horse with a blocked leg or an elephant with a blocked eye has fewer moves. If you also lock your general inside the palace with your own advisors, you may be one move from a loss.
3. **Keep a spare soldier move.** A soldier that can still step forward is a free move you can use to avoid running out of moves.
4. **When you are the attacker, check before you capture.** Make sure that your last quiet move does not accidentally leave your opponent with a free move, and that it is not you who gets stalemated.
