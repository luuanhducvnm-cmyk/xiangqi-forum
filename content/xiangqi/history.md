Xiangqi is old, widely played, and surprisingly hard to date. This page sticks to what is reasonably well established and flags what is uncertain.

## Origins: a debated picture

The chess family of games is generally thought to descend from an ancient Indian game, chaturanga, which spread west to Persia and the Arab world (becoming shatranj) and east along trade routes. How xiangqi fits into that story is **debated**. Some historians think it grew from the same ancestor; others argue that board games with elephant, chariot and horse pieces were developed in China independently or in parallel.

What is clear is that:

- References to "elephant" games (象戲, *xiàngxì*) appear in Chinese writing for more than a thousand years.
- The game's design changed significantly over time; early versions did not look like the modern game.
- By the time of the Song dynasty (960–1279) a game recognisable as xiangqi — with a river, palaces and cannons — is generally thought to have existed, though details are debated.

## The river and the theme

The words 楚河漢界 ("Chu river, Han border") across the middle of the board refer to the war between the states of Chu and Han (about 206–202 BC). The two armies face each other across the river; the generals are confined to their palaces; the pieces are chariots, horses, cannons and soldiers — the elements of an ancient army. It is a theme rather than a proven origin story.

## The modern game

Over several centuries the rules settled into the form played today: 9×10 board, palace and river, the cannon with its jump-capture, and the flying-general rule. The 20th century brought:

- Standard tournament rules and organised competitions, including international events;
- Chess clocks and notation systems ([notation](/xiangqi/notation/));
- Large communities of players in China, Vietnam, Singapore, Malaysia and Chinese communities worldwide.

Today xiangqi is among the most widely played board games in the world, and you can play it online in your browser — including in 3D at [Xiangqi 3D](https://xiangqi3d.online).
