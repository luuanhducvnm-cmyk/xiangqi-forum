This page gives the rules of xiangqi in the form used by most players and by [Xiangqi 3D](https://xiangqi3d.online). Rule sets published by the World Xiangqi Federation, the Chinese federation and the Asian federations agree on all the basics; they differ mainly in how they treat repeated positions, so those are covered separately below.

## Setup

Red and Black each place 16 pieces on the intersections of a 9×10 board. The back rank, from the corner, is chariot, horse, elephant, advisor, general, advisor, elephant, horse, chariot. The cannons stand on the third rank in front of the horses, and the five soldiers stand on the fourth rank on every other file. See the [board and setup page](/xiangqi/board-setup/).

## Turns

Players alternate moves; Red moves first. On your turn you must move one piece. You may not pass. A piece may only move to an empty point or to a point occupied by an enemy piece, which is captured and removed.

## How the pieces move

| Piece | Movement |
|---|---|
| General | One point orthogonally, never leaving the palace |
| Advisor | One point diagonally, never leaving the palace |
| Elephant | Two points diagonally; cannot cross the river; blocked if the point in between is occupied |
| Horse | One point orthogonally then one diagonally outward; blocked if the orthogonal point is occupied |
| Chariot | Any distance orthogonally, not jumping over pieces |
| Cannon | Moves like a chariot; captures by jumping over exactly one piece (the screen) to land on the next piece |
| Soldier | One point forward; once across the river also one point sideways; never backward |

## Check, checkmate and stalemate

A general is **in check** when an enemy piece could capture it on the next move. A player in check must get out of check immediately, by one of three means:

1. Capture the attacking piece.
2. Block the line of attack (against a horse, by occupying the point in front of it — its "leg").
3. Move the general to a point that is not attacked.

A player may never make a move that leaves their own general in check. If a player is in check and none of the three responses is possible, that player is **checkmated** and loses.

> **Stalemate is a loss.** If it is your turn, you are not in check, and you have no legal move, you lose. Positions where one side runs out of moves are therefore a winning goal, not a draw.

## The flying-general rule

The two generals may never stand on the same file with no piece between them (see also the [flying general rule explained](/xiangqi/flying-general-rule/)). Because of this:

- A move that opens the file between the generals is illegal for the player making it.
- A piece standing alone between the generals is **pinned** to that file: it can move along the file, but not off it.
- Generals therefore also control their file: an enemy general can never step onto a file where yours is facing it with nothing between.

## Perpetual check and chasing

If a position repeats, the result depends on who is responsible for the repetition.

- **Perpetual check** (giving check on every move): the player giving perpetual check is not allowed to continue and must vary the play; if only one side is checking, that side is the one that loses or must change.
- **Perpetual chase** (threatening the same piece or pieces again and again with captures that cannot be realised): most official rules also forbid it, with exceptions for threats to unprotected soldiers.
- **Mutual repetition** with neither side responsible is a draw.

These rules are complicated and differ slightly between the World Xiangqi Federation, the Chinese and the Asian rule sets. Xiangqi 3D uses a simplified version: if exactly one side is giving check (or chasing) throughout a threefold repetition, that side loses; otherwise the game is drawn.

## Ending the game

For the stalemate rule in detail, read [stalemate in xiangqi](/xiangqi/stalemate/).

- **Checkmate**, or the opponent having **no legal move**.
- **Resignation** by either player.
- **Time loss**: the player whose clock runs out loses (in casual play).
- **Draw by agreement**: either player may offer a draw; if the opponent accepts, the game is drawn.
- **Draw by repetition** where neither side is at fault.
- **Draw by no progress**: after a long sequence with no captures (commonly 60 moves per side) the game is drawn.
- **Draw because no side can mate** — for example when both sides have only generals and defensive pieces.

## Etiquette and conventions

- Red usually plays from the bottom of the board; if you play Black, many online boards (including Xiangqi 3D) turn the board around so that you are at the bottom.
- In over-the-board play, "touch-move" applies: a piece you touch must be moved if it can be.
- Games are normally timed. Xiangqi 3D uses a real two-button clock so that the rhythm of play feels like a physical game.
