Everybody loses pieces in their first xiangqi games. The good news: nearly all beginner losses come from the same handful of mistakes. Fix these nine and your results will improve quickly.

## 1. Forgetting the horse's leg

A horse first steps one point straight, then one point diagonally. If a piece sits on that first step, the horse cannot jump in that direction. Beginners plan a horse attack and discover the horse is blocked by their own soldier.

{{img:xq-horse-block|The Red horse is hobbled: pieces directly above and to the right block four of its jumps.}}

**Fix:** before planning a horse jump, check the four points directly next to the horse.

## 2. Leaving the chariots at home

The chariot is the strongest piece, but only when it has open lines. Leaving it in the corner for ten moves hands the initiative to your opponent.

**Fix:** develop both chariots early. Moving a cannon or a horse first often frees the corner chariot. See [openings](/xiangqi/openings/).

## 3. Using the cannon without a screen

A cannon cannot capture without exactly one piece in between. In an empty board it is much weaker, and in a crowded one it can be trapped. Beginners often move a cannon forward expecting it to attack, and it simply sits there.

**Fix:** think of the cannon as a *two-piece team*: the cannon plus its screen. Read the [cannon page](/xiangqi/pieces/cannon/).

## 4. Ignoring the flying general

The two generals may never face each other on an open file. This rule decides many endgames, and beginners also use it against themselves, moving a piece off the file and leaving their own general exposed.

{{img:xq-flying|The Red chariot is pinned: if it leaves the central file the generals face each other.}}

**Fix:** before moving a piece on the general's file, check whether it is the last piece between the generals. See the [flying general rule](/xiangqi/flying-general-rule/).

## 5. Pushing soldiers carelessly

Soldiers move only forward until they cross the river, and they cannot retreat. A soldier pushed too early can become stuck, and a soldier that crosses without support is easily captured.

{{img:xq-soldier-after|A soldier that has crossed the river can step forward or sideways.}}

**Fix:** advance soldiers with a purpose (to open a file or to support an attack) and save them for the endgame when possible.

## 6. Throwing away the advisors and elephants

Advisors and elephants cannot attack far, so beginners treat them as useless and move them away. But they protect the general in the endgame, and elephants stop soldiers and horses from crossing.

**Fix:** keep both advisors and elephants near the palace until the endgame is clear. Remember that elephants cannot cross the river; see the [river and palace guide](/xiangqi/river-and-palace/).

## 7. Trading evenly without a plan

Swapping a chariot for a chariot is fine; swapping a chariot for a horse and a soldier is not. Use [piece values](/xiangqi/piece-values/) as a quick check: chariot ≈ 9, cannon ≈ 4½, horse ≈ 4, advisor and elephant ≈ 2 each, soldier ≈ 1 (more after the river).

## 8. Missing one-move threats

Most blunders are not deep: a piece is left capturable, or a cheap piece attacks a valuable one. Before each move ask: **what does my opponent threaten now, and what will they threaten after my move?** Practise with the patterns in [xiangqi tactics](/xiangqi/tactics/).

## 9. Running out of time

On a clock, thinking too long in the opening leaves too little for the endgame. Play the opening quickly with a plan and save your time for critical positions. The [time control guide](/chess/time-controls/) explains how clocks work in both games.

## Practise the fixes

Play a few games against the Royal AI at the easiest level. Do not worry about winning; concentrate on checking horse legs, protecting your chariots and watching the generals.
