Two features of the xiangqi board decide how the pieces behave: the **river** and the **palace**. Once you understand them, most movement rules become easy to remember.

{{img:xq-board|The xiangqi board: nine files by ten ranks, a palace for each side (highlighted) and the river across the middle.}}

## The river (楚河漢界)

The river is the empty band across the middle of the board. Pieces stand on the *points* (the line crossings) on either side of it; there is no point in the river itself, so pieces simply move across it.

The traditional characters written in it, 楚河 (*Chǔ River*) and 漢界 (*Han border*), refer to the war between the Chu and Han kingdoms that gave the game its theme.

What the river changes:

| Piece | Effect of the river |
|---|---|
| Elephant | Can **never cross** it. It defends only its own half. |
| Soldier | Moves forward only before crossing; **after crossing** it may also move one point sideways. |
| Chariot, cannon, horse | No restriction. They cross freely. |
| Advisor, general | Stay in the palace, so the river does not matter for them. |

{{img:xq-elephant-river|A Red elephant on the river bank can only retreat; its forward jumps would cross the river.}}

## The palace (九宮)

Each side has a 3×3 block of nine points marked with two diagonal lines. This is the palace.

- The **general** may move one point at a time, but only along the lines and only **inside the palace**.
- The two **advisors** move one point diagonally and also stay **inside the palace**.
- Other pieces may enter the palace freely, to attack the general.

The palace is small, and that is what makes xiangqi endgames sharp: a general with two advisors is protected but also boxed in, which is why a chariot and a cannon can checkmate so quickly. See [checkmate patterns](/xiangqi/checkmate-patterns/).

## Crossing the river with soldiers

Soldiers are weak at the start, because they only move forward one point. After crossing, they gain a sideways step, which makes them useful attackers close to the enemy general.

{{img:xq-soldier-before|Before the river: a soldier can only step forward one point.}}

{{img:xq-soldier-after|After the river: a soldier can step forward or one point sideways.}}

Soldiers never retreat, so a soldier on the last rank can only move sideways. Use that fact when you plan endgames: advanced soldiers can control the squares next to the enemy general.

## The flying general across the river

The generals may never face each other on the same file with no piece between them. This often happens when a piece that was blocking the file moves away, so many beginners blunder here. Read the [flying general rule](/xiangqi/flying-general-rule/).

## Quick summary

- River: elephants stop; soldiers become stronger.
- Palace: general and advisors stay inside.
- Everything else moves freely.
