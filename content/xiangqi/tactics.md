Most xiangqi games under about 1500 rating are decided by tactics: a threat the opponent did not see. The patterns below cover most of them.

## The three questions

Before every move ask:

1. **What are all the opponent's checks?** (If the opponent can give check, can it be dangerous?)
2. **What can the opponent capture?** (Is a piece loose?)
3. **What does the opponent threaten after my move?**

## Fork (double attack)

A fork is one move that attacks two pieces at once. The opponent can only save one.

- **Horse forks** are the most common: a horse jumps to a point that attacks, for example, a chariot and a cannon. Remember the horse leg: the fork only works if the leg is clear ([horse page](/xiangqi/pieces/horse/)).
- **Chariot forks** along a rank or file attack two loose pieces.
- **Check forks** (check plus attack): giving check with a piece that also attacks another is the strongest form, because the opponent must answer the check.

## Pin

A pin freezes a piece because moving it would expose something more valuable behind it.

- **The flying-general pin.** A piece standing alone between the two generals cannot leave the file ([rules](/xiangqi/rules/)).
- **Chariot pins** against a general, with the pinned piece unable to move without losing the piece behind it.
- **Cannon pins.** A cannon aimed through a screen at a valuable piece pins the screen — if the screen moves, the cannon's line becomes dangerous.

## Discovered attack

Move a piece out of the way so that a piece *behind* it attacks. This is especially strong with the cannon: when the screen moves, the cannon lines up on a new target. If the moving piece also makes a threat, you have a **double attack**.

## Skewer

Attack a valuable piece in such a way that when it moves, you capture a lesser piece behind it. Chariots on open files skewer a general and a chariot, or a chariot and a cannon.

## Deflection and overloading

- **Deflection** pulls a defender away from what it protects.
- **Overloading** gives a defender too many jobs: an advisor guarding two points can only guard one.

## Sacrifices

Giving up a piece for a stronger attack is normal in xiangqi. Good sacrifices open lines to the general and lead to forced checkmate ([patterns](/xiangqi/checkmate-patterns/)). Always check that the attack is forced before sacrificing; a speculative sacrifice that fails loses.

## Chasing and tempo

In xiangqi, moving a piece with a threat on every move gains time (a **tempo**) and can drive the opponent to bad squares. There are rules limiting perpetual chasing ([rules](/xiangqi/rules/)), but short chases are a normal part of strategy.

## How to practise tactics

- Look at each opponent move and ask "what is threatened now?".
- After each game, review the move where material was lost.
- Play online: [Xiangqi 3D](https://xiangqi3d.online) makes it easy to try patterns against the Royal AI.
