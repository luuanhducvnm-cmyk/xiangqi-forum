If you play chess, you already know most of what xiangqi asks. This page shows what is the same, what is different, and what to unlearn.

## At a glance

| | Chess | Xiangqi |
|---|---|---|
| Board | 8×8 squares | 9×10 points (pieces on intersections) |
| Pieces per side | 16 | 16 |
| First move | White | Red |
| King / general | Moves one square in any direction; can castle | Moves one point orthogonally; confined to the palace |
| Queen | Yes | No (no equivalent) |
| Rook / chariot | Rook | Chariot — same movement |
| Bishop / elephant | Slides diagonally | Moves exactly 2 diagonally; can be blocked; cannot cross the river |
| Knight / horse | Jumps over pieces | Moves in an L but is blocked by an adjacent piece |
| Cannon | No equivalent | Moves like a chariot; captures by jumping one piece |
| Pawn / soldier | Forward, captures diagonally, promotes | Forward (then sideways after the river), no promotion |
| Special moves | Castling, en passant, promotion | None |
| Stalemate | Draw | **Loss** for the stalemated player |
| Defending zone | Anywhere | Palace for the general and advisors |

## The big ideas that change

### Stalemate is a loss

In chess, running out of legal moves means a draw. In xiangqi it means you lose. Positions where the opponent is nearly out of moves are good for the attacker.

### There is no queen

The most powerful xiangqi piece is the chariot, which is "only" a rook. Material imbalances you know from chess do not apply directly — see the [pieces overview](/xiangqi/pieces/) for rough values.

### The flying-general rule

Generals may not face each other down an open file. In chess terms, that means the king cannot stand on a file with the opposing king when nothing is between — and a piece caught between them is pinned. Details in [the rules](/xiangqi/rules/).

### The cannon

This is the one truly new piece. It takes a few games to get used to threats through a screen. Start with [the cannon page](/xiangqi/pieces/cannon/).

### Blocked moves

In chess a knight leaps over anything. In xiangqi, the horse can be hobbled by a piece on its leg, and the elephant by a piece on its eye. Always check the leg before you plan a horse jump.

## Strategy: what carries over

- **Development matters** — get your chariots and horses out, as you would knights and bishops.
- **Control of open lines** — chariots on open files are like rooks on open files.
- **Tempo and initiative** — the first move is a real advantage in both games.
- **King safety** — in xiangqi that means keeping advisors and elephants close.

## What to unlearn

- You cannot castle, so you must bring the chariots out yourself.
- Pawns/soldiers do not promote; trade them off for concrete gains.
- A lone attacking piece can often not mate, because defenders sit inside the palace.
- Cannons and horses are stronger than you may expect in attacking positions.

## Learning both

Playing both games sharpens your tactics in each: chess trains long-range piece coordination; xiangqi trains attacks against a confined king. You can play both in 3D in the browser: [Xiangqi 3D](https://xiangqi3d.online) and [Chess 3D](https://chess3d.online). If you want to see how they compare, read [how to play chess](/chess/how-to-play/) and [how to play xiangqi](/xiangqi/how-to-play/).
