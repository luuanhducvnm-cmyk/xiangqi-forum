The flying general rule is the one special rule in xiangqi that has no match in chess. It is also a source of surprising pins and mates, so it is worth learning early.

## What the rule says

The two generals may **never stand on the same file** (the same vertical line) **with no piece between them**. If a move would leave them like that, the move is illegal.

Think of it as the generals being able to "see" each other down an open file: that is never allowed, so any move that would open the line is simply not a legal move.

## Three consequences

1. **A lone piece between the generals is pinned.** Whether it belongs to you or to your opponent, it can move along the file but not off it, because that would leave the generals facing each other.
2. **A general controls its file.** An enemy general cannot step onto a file where your general faces it with nothing in between.
3. **Flight squares disappear.** When a general is under attack, the squares on the other general's file are not available to it, which makes mating nets smaller than they look.

## How to use it

- **Pin a piece against the file.** If an enemy piece stands alone between the generals, attack it: it cannot run away sideways.
- **Take away flight squares.** Place your general so that it controls a file next to the enemy general. The enemy general cannot step onto that file while nothing blocks it.
- **Add it to a mating net.** When you count the escape squares of an enemy general, remember the squares it cannot use because of the rule. Combined with a chariot or cannon, this often decides a [checkmate](/xiangqi/checkmate-patterns/).

## How to defend against it

- **Be careful with a lone piece between the generals.** It is pinned and cannot leave the file, so it can become a target.
- **Keep two blockers on the file** when you can, so the line stays blocked even if one of them is captured or has to move.
- **Count the flight squares** each general has, including those taken away by the rule, before you plan an attack or defence.
