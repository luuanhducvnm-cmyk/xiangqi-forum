The **Royal AI** is the built-in opponent in both [Chess 3D](https://chess3d.online) and [Xiangqi 3D](https://xiangqi3d.online). It is always available, never gets tired, and is a good way to learn.

{{cta:both}}

## How the AI counts for your rating

- The rating system treats the Royal AI as a fixed opponent of about **1300**.
- Games against it count at **half value** — wins and losses both move your rating less.
- They **stop adding points above 1500**; beyond that you must beat people to climb.
- Very fast games, and too many in a short time, may not count; this keeps ratings fair.

See [ELO ratings explained](/elo-rating-explained/) for how the numbers work.

## A simple practice plan

1. **Play a game** and finish it, even if you are losing — endgames teach a lot.
2. **Review the game**: find the first moment you lost material or the initiative.
3. **Practise one idea** per session: an opening, a tactic, a mating pattern.
4. **Mix it up**: after a few AI games, try a human opponent.

Resources: for chess, see [strategy](/chess/strategy/), [tactics](/chess/tactics/) and [checkmates](/chess/checkmates/); for xiangqi, [strategy](/xiangqi/strategy/), [tactics](/xiangqi/tactics/) and [checkmate patterns](/xiangqi/checkmate-patterns/).

## Tips

- Start each game by making a plan for the opening ([chess openings](/chess/openings/), [xiangqi openings](/xiangqi/openings/)).
- Use the clock: you do not have to play fast, but get used to the rhythm.
- Try playing the colour you find hardest — Black in chess, or Red/Black in xiangqi.
