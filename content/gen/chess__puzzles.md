Checkmate puzzles are the fastest way to improve at chess: they train you to see forcing lines, count escape squares and calculate every defence. These sets go from **mate in 1** to **mate in 3**, with a hint and a full solution for every position.

## Why puzzles work

A mate puzzle forces you to do three things every game needs: list the checking moves, count the squares the enemy king can use, and test each defence to the end. Do a few every day and these steps become automatic. Begin with mate in 1 to learn to recognise final patterns, then use mate in 2 and 3 to practise sacrifices and quiet moves, where the key is often not a check.

## The puzzle sets

- [Chess Mate in 1 Puzzles](/chess/puzzles/mate-in-1/): Six one-move checkmates: find the only move that mates.
- [Chess Mate in 2 Puzzles](/chess/puzzles/mate-in-2/): Eight two-move checkmates, each with one key move and every defence refuted.
- [Chess Mate in 3 Puzzles](/chess/puzzles/mate-in-3/): Six three-move checkmates: calculate deeper and find the single winning start.

## How we make sure the answers are right

Each puzzle was generated by computer and proven with the Chess 3D rules engine: exactly one winning first move, and every defence solved to checkmate. Solutions are produced from the engine's search tree, not typed by hand, so they cannot contain a mistake in the notation or a missed defence.
