Xiangqi puzzles teach you how chariots, cannons and horses combine to trap a general in the palace. These sets go from **mate in 1** to **mate in 3**, each with a hint and a full solution.

## Why puzzles work

In xiangqi the general is boxed into a small palace, so most wins come from a few repeating mating ideas: a chariot on an open file, a cannon needing a screen, a horse covering the escape squares, and the flying-general rule closing the last gap. Solving puzzles lets you see these ideas many times in a short period. Start with mate in 1 to learn how each piece delivers the final check, then work through mate in 2 and 3, where you must also find which defences exist and answer each of them.

## The puzzle sets

- [Xiangqi Mate in 1 Puzzles](/xiangqi/puzzles/mate-in-1/): Six one-move checkmates: find the only move that mates the general.
- [Xiangqi Mate in 2 Puzzles](/xiangqi/puzzles/mate-in-2/): Eight two-move checkmates, each with a single key move and every defence refuted.
- [Xiangqi Mate in 3 Puzzles](/xiangqi/puzzles/mate-in-3/): Hard three-move checkmates: calculate deeper and find the single winning start.

## How we make sure the answers are right

Each puzzle was generated by computer and proven with the Xiangqi 3D rules engine, flying-general rule included: exactly one winning first move, and every defence solved to a real checkmate. Solutions come from the engine's search tree, not from memory.
